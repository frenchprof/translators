Why it matters

French administrations reject most foreign certifications.

Prefectures, CPAM and town halls require translations by an expert registered with a French Court of Appeal. Translations certified by UK or US agencies are routinely refused, which costs weeks. Ours are listed with the embassies and accepted the first time.

FAQ

Common questions

What is a sworn translation?

A sworn translation is certified by a traducteur assermenté, a judicial expert who has taken an oath before a court. Important: French prefectures and administrations (CPAM, Mairie, etc.) strictly require translators registered with a French Court of Appeal. Certifications from UK or US agencies are usually rejected for official French use.

How long does it take?

Standard turnaround is 3 to 5 business days. Urgent 24-hour service is available for most single-page documents.

Which embassies accept your translations?

We are officially listed with the French Embassy and the Swiss Embassy in Singapore. Our translations are also accepted by MOM, ICA, Singapore courts and most international institutions.

How do I send my documents?

Send scans or photos via WhatsApp or email. For originals that need a physical stamp, we arrange secure collection or drop-off in Singapore.

What are your rates?

Rates depend on document type, language pair, length and urgency. Quotes are free and without obligation.

Are my documents confidential?

Absolutely. All documents are handled with strict professional confidentiality and never shared with third parties.

Do my translations need an apostille or legalisation?

It depends on the destination. For use in France: foreign documents usually need an apostille from their country of origin before translation. For French documents used abroad: since May 2025, apostilles in France are issued by notaries, no longer by the Courts of Appeal. We guide you through this new process. Within the EU: under Regulation 2016/1191, many civil documents (birth and marriage certificates) are exempt from legalisation. For Singapore authorities (ICA, MOM, courts) no apostille is needed; our sworn PDF translations are accepted directly.
